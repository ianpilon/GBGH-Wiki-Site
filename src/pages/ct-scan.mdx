# CT Scan

**Summary**: Computed Tomography uses X-rays and computers to create 3D images, sometimes with injected dye. Most CT exams take 5-20 minutes. Located in Diagnostic Imaging on the main level. Pre-scan preparation depends on the body part being scanned.

**Sources**: `pages/programs-and-services-diagnostic-imaging-ct-information-for-patients-and-family.md`, news post "GBGH's new CT scanner makes a big entrance"

**Last updated**: 2026-05-25

---

## What CT is

X-rays and computers combine to create realistic 3D images. Sometimes contrast dye is injected into a vein. Images reviewed by a Radiologist for diagnosis.

## Where and duration

- Main level, near the Emergency Department.
- Most scans: ~15 minutes; range 5-20 minutes.
- DI Reception: 705-526-1300 ext. 5090.

(source: `pages/programs-and-services-diagnostic-imaging-ct-information-for-patients-and-family.md`)

## Preparation by exam type

### Head, chest, neck, or spine

- No jewelry or metal artefacts (zippers, metal buttons, underwires) over the area.
- May change into hospital gown.

### Abdomen or pelvis (with contrast)

- Pick up oral contrast in Diagnostic Imaging at least 2 days before (you will be told if needed); alternatively, drink 1L water over 60-90 minutes.
- Nothing to eat or drink for 2 hours before appointment (except the oral contrast or water).
- No metal artefacts over the area.
- Expect to be in the department 1-2 hours; ~15 minutes in the scan room.

### Important to disclose on arrival

- Allergies, kidney problems, diabetes, possible pregnancy.

## During and after

- Possible IV line in arm for contrast.
- Asked to hold still and hold breath for ~15 seconds during image capture.
- Scan table moves through the donut-shaped scanner.
- ~15 minutes total scan time.
- If dye was used: remain in the department 15 minutes after.
- Results sent to ordering physician.
